A social and philosophical inquiry

Decimation killed one soldier in ten.
We end three pregnancies in ten.

Every age faces hard choices when urgent needs and obligations to vulnerable life collide. This project sets the modern abortion debate beside the historic record of child sacrifice and asks what a society owes its next generation.

How many abortions occur worldwide?

The published global model estimates 73.3 million induced abortions per year on average in 2015–2019, with an 80% uncertainty interval of 66.7 to 82.0 million. WHO’s December 2025 fact sheet cites this work. The date of the fact sheet and the years analyzed are different. The numbers count abortions, so someone who had more than one can contribute more than one event. Model supplement, Tables 4–5 · WHO fact sheet

Each point is an annual average over five years. The shaded areas show the model’s 80% uncertainty intervals. The estimated count rose over the published series, while the rate per 1,000 was similar at the start and end.

Costs, jobs and taxes

  • Households. Count the out-of-pocket bill, travel, child care and unpaid work time. A U.S. patient study measured these expenses and found substantial financial burdens, especially for people traveling out of state. Patient expense study
  • Jobs and earnings. Track time away from work, employment and earnings separately. One Turnaway analysis found less full-time employment initially among women who gave birth after denial. A separate credit-record analysis found sustained financial distress after denial, with no strong evidence of employment changes in its linked sample. These studies answer questions about U.S. patients seeking care near facility limits. Employment and poverty study · Credit-record study
  • Public budgets. Report what a program pays, who funds it, and whether the policy changes other spending or tax receipts. A net estimate needs a defined jurisdiction, policy comparison and time horizon. Future births, later childbearing, earnings and public costs all affect such a model. The project has no global net tax or jobs estimate. Public funding records · Policy studies
  • Health-system costs. Guttmacher’s calculator estimates about $1.56 billion annually to treat complications of unsafe abortion across the 117 countries it covers. This is a modeled component of spending. The scope excludes household time, other medical services and any proposed tax effect. Calculator methodology · Published cost estimate

Measured outcomes for women and families

National abortion counts track ended pregnancies. The figures below address health and other outcomes for people who received or sought care. Individual accounts of grief, relief and coercion can each be real. To estimate an average effect, researchers need a comparison group that accounts for why people sought an abortion and their circumstances before it.

  • Physical safety. The CDC estimated 0.46 deaths related to legal induced abortion per 100,000 reported legal abortions in the United States during 2013–2021. This is a case-fatality rate for legal procedures. It does not measure deaths from unsafe abortion, the risks of continuing a pregnancy, or the number of pregnancies ended. CDC abortion surveillance, Table 15
  • Mental health. The National Academies’ 2018 review concluded that having an abortion does not increase the risk of depression, anxiety or PTSD. A U.S. study followed 956 people who sought abortions. Those denied care near facility gestational limits had more anxiety initially and similar depression scores. Psychological well-being improved over time and the groups converged. These findings describe groups and leave room for individual experiences. A separate five-year analysis of people who received an abortion found no increase in decision regret over time in its sample. Decision difficulty and stigma were associated with more negative emotions. Five-year emotions study · National Academies evidence review · Five-year study · Indexed abstract
  • Future fertility. The National Academies review found no increased risk of secondary infertility in the evidence it reviewed. It found insufficient well-designed research to draw broad conclusions about later ectopic pregnancy, miscarriage or stillbirth. Wanting a child later, being able to conceive and wishing an earlier pregnancy had continued are separate questions. The Turnaway follow-up documented later births and asked about pregnancy intentions. It gives no population-wide estimate of how many people later wish they had continued the earlier pregnancy. Evidence review · Subsequent-birth study
  • Prior abortions and later pregnancies. In 41 U.S. reporting areas in 2022, 56.1% of abortions with known history were among patients without a prior induced abortion. This describes reported procedures. The National Academies review identified an association between two or more prior aspiration abortions and the rare outcome of a first birth before 28 weeks. The finding concerns a specific procedure and outcome. It does not establish the same risk for all methods or a causal effect for an individual patient. CDC surveillance, Table 9 · Evidence review
  • Costs and policy effects. The project also tracks household financial distress after denial, changes in births after bans, and estimates of travel and other costs. Each estimate depends on its population and comparison. Household evidence · Policy consequences
  • Coercion and trafficking. Court records document traffickers coercing individual victims to end pregnancies. Establishing whether access made trafficking easier would require a credible comparison across settings and a measure of coercion or trafficking. National abortion surveillance and the cases in this archive cannot answer that question. Case evidence · Federal trafficking data limitations

Decimation, tripled

Roman decimation was a punishment imposed on a military unit. One soldier in ten was selected to die, sometimes at the hands of fellow soldiers. The word still carries the horror of a community made to pay for its failure of virtue. Taylor on Roman decimation.

Decimation took one life in ten within a punished unit. WHO estimates that 29 in 100 pregnancies worldwide ended in induced abortion in 2015 to 2019. The thesis this project explores is that a society can lose a generation at scale without ever feeling it as a loss, because the loss is private, dispersed and unnamed. WHO abortion estimates.

Where the comparison strains. Commanders imposed decimation on unwilling soldiers. Abortion is a personal decision shaped by particular circumstances. Whether an embryo or fetus has the moral standing of a soldier is the very question in dispute.

The record of sacrifice

Some accounts describe child sacrifice as an appeal for rain or divine favor. This project asks what that history can tell us about obligations to vulnerable life when present needs press.

  1. Carthage, first millennium BCE. The Carthage Tophet held urns containing cremated remains of infants and young children. Researchers disagree over how much of the evidence points to sacrifice and how much is consistent with natural deaths. Smith and colleagues, Schwartz and colleagues.
  2. Ancient Israel. Leviticus forbids offering children to Molech, and Jeremiah condemns child sacrifice at Tophet in the Valley of Hinnom. The texts leave open how often such offerings occurred. Leviticus 18, Jeremiah 7.
  3. Huanchaquito-Las Llamas, Peru, 15th century. Excavators found 137 children, three adults and more than 200 camelids. The research team suggested that the sacrifice may have been a response to severe rains and flooding. Prieto and colleagues.
  4. Tlatelolco, Aztec Mexico. A study published in 2008 reported that excavations at a temple dedicated to the Aztec god of wind and rain recovered the remains of 43 sacrificial victims, 37 of them children or adolescents. De La Cruz and colleagues.
Where the comparison strains. The children in these cases had already been born. Most abortions reported in U.S. surveillance occur early in pregnancy. The modern debate also asks whether the state may require someone to remain pregnant. CDC abortion surveillance.

The strongest case against this page

The comparison with historic child sacrifice puts the focus on the life that ends. Pregnancy also requires the sustained use of another person's body. Even if a fetus has the same right to life as a born person, the bodily autonomy argument asks whether that right entitles it to remain inside someone's body against their will.

Judith Jarvis Thomson asks you to imagine waking up connected, without your consent, to an unconscious violinist with a kidney ailment. Your kidneys will keep him alive while he recovers. If you disconnect before nine months, he will die. Thomson argues that you may disconnect even though the violinist has a right to life. The analogy is especially forceful when pregnancy results from rape. For consensual sex, Thomson imagines an airborne seed that can grow into a person taking root in your home despite a protective screen. Her point is that taking a foreseeable risk can leave your right to refuse bodily support intact. A Defense of Abortion.

In the United States, living organ donation generally requires consent, even when a life depends on it. In McFall v. Shimp, a Pennsylvania court refused to order a compatible relative to donate bone marrow. The autonomy argument calls for the same protection against compelled bodily support during pregnancy. Federal informed consent regulation, Court decision.

Pregnancy and childbirth can cause severe bleeding and lasting harm. When the membranes rupture very early in pregnancy, waiting can increase the risk of serious infection. How to manage the pregnancy depends on the medical circumstances and the patient's preferences. A fetal diagnosis can bring a prognosis of death before or soon after birth, sometimes with substantial uncertainty. The case for autonomy gives the person facing these risks and decisions the authority to decide what they can bear. Maternal health risks, Early rupture of membranes, Life-limiting fetal diagnoses.

The parental-duty reply holds that parents owe their children care beyond what strangers owe each other. If that duty begins before birth, gestation may count as the basic care a fetus needs at that stage. The responsibility reply makes a separate claim. When people voluntarily take a foreseeable risk and thereby bring a dependent life into existence, they can acquire duties even if they tried to prevent pregnancy. That argument relies on a voluntary act, so it falls away in cases of rape. Some pro-life thinkers still oppose abortion after rape because they see the fetus's claim to protection as independent of how conception occurred. In medical emergencies, some argue that treatment aimed at saving the pregnant person's life can be permissible even when fetal death is a foreseeable consequence. Parental obligation and responsibility, The Ethics of Abortion, The doctrine of double effect.

Don Marquis argues that a central wrong of killing is that it deprives someone of the relationships, experiences and projects their future would have held. If a fetus already has a future like ours, abortion deprives it of that future. For Marquis, this gives a strong moral reason against abortion without first having to establish fetal personhood. Why Abortion Is Immoral.

This project takes seriously the possibility that the parental relationship creates duties before birth. Gestation calls for a degree of bodily support that ordinary caregiving rarely does, and adoption can transfer care only after childbirth. Any case for compelling pregnancy in law must explain why that duty justifies the burdens it places on the person who is pregnant and how clinicians can exercise judgment when a pregnancy threatens health or life.

What remains unresolved. What moral claims does prenatal life have at different stages? Can a parental duty require gestation, and how should rape, a life-limiting fetal diagnosis or serious health risks affect that duty? Even where a moral duty exists, when may the state enforce it, and who should decide how much medical risk is too much?

U.S. abortion history, 1969 to 2025

The Guttmacher historical estimates cover 1973 to 2020. The earliest CDC surveillance starts in 1969 with limited reporting. CDC counts cover the jurisdictions that reported each year. The break in 1998 marks a coverage change that included the loss of California from the series. CDC 2005, CDC 2014, CDC 2022.

The Guttmacher monthly study estimates clinician-provided abortions, including telehealth under shield laws, for 2023 to 2025. Its estimates exclude self-managed abortions outside the formal health system and abortions provided under exceptions in states with total bans. Family, fragility and the thing we cannot measure

The strongest evidence here concerns connection. A meta-analysis of 148 studies and over 300,000 people found that those with stronger social relationships had 50% greater odds of survival. One British panel study found that married participants reported higher life satisfaction after accounting for their well-being before marriage. Holt-Lunstad and colleagues, Grover and Helliwell.

Supportive marriages and extended families can give people someone to share a shock with and someone willing to tell them the truth. Friendships, work and community commitments can also help people learn to bear hardship. The case this project makes is that supportive family ties can build resilience under stress.

What families and communities teach

Family life can teach people how to keep a promise when keeping it is inconvenient. In the Politics, Aristotle treats the household as an association that meets everyday needs. In the Nicomachean Ethics, he argues that moral virtue develops through practice. Taken together, these ideas suggest why the ordinary work of raising children, caring for elders, and making amends matters. Aristotle defended male authority and slavery within the household. His account of how habits form can also be put to work in households whose members have equal standing. 1, 2

The Confucian Analects treats care and respect for parents as part of moral cultivation. It also counsels people to raise objections gently when they disagree with their parents. In Reflections on the Revolution in France, Edmund Burke describes political society as a partnership that joins the living with their predecessors and with people yet to be born. That view asks us to consider what future generations will inherit alongside what people need today. 3, 4

In Suicide, Émile Durkheim argued that weaker integration into family, religious, and political groups helped explain differences in suicide rates across populations. Alexis de Tocqueville saw voluntary associations as a way for people in a democracy to learn to act together. In his 1995 essay Bowling Alone, Robert D. Putnam documented declines in many forms of American civic participation in the late twentieth century. A bowling league illustrates what is at stake. Meeting regularly gives people a chance to build trust and learn who will show up when needed. 5, 6, 7

These ties can help people absorb a shock. Nassim Nicholas Taleb calls a system antifragile when it gains from variation or stress. Take a household where an illness prompts relatives and neighbors to share school pickups. If the arrangement leaves the household with more people it can call on in the next crisis, it illustrates antifragility. If the same work repeatedly falls to one exhausted caregiver, the household may have coped while leaving that person more vulnerable. 8

Susan Moller Okin argues that assigning most unpaid care to women can curb their earning power and leave them dependent on a partner. John Stuart Mill's On Liberty warns that social pressure can restrict freedom and argues that preventing harm to others is what justifies coercion of adults. A defense of family life must reckon with who bears the costs of care and leave room for people to dissent from the roles others expect them to fill. 9, 10

These questions carry into the abortion debate. Many opponents of abortion see protecting prenatal life as a duty to a vulnerable human being. Many defenders of abortion access emphasize bodily autonomy and the burdens of being compelled to continue a pregnancy. An ethic of care across generations leaves open how to weigh those claims in law. It gives people on both sides a reason to ask who will share the work of caring for children and what makes that care possible.

Public money and public conscience

Should people be taxed to pay for something they believe is the taking of a life? Since fiscal year 1977 the Hyde Amendment has largely barred federal Medicaid funds from paying for abortion. Its exceptions cover rape, incest and danger to the pregnant patient's life. Congress restored the rape and incest exceptions in 1993. States may fund broader coverage with their own revenue. Congressional Research Service, KFF state policy analysis.

How money reaches providers

Medicaid and CHIP pay for covered care through state programs, often through managed care plans, using federal and state funds. States can use their own money for broader coverage. Grants pay for work defined by the award. Title X is one example. Its statute bars use of Title X funds in programs where abortion is a method of family planning. A provider may also receive payments from private insurers, patients, and donors. GAO funding overview, pp. 4–6, Title X statute.

For 2019–2021, GAO estimated that Planned Parenthood affiliates received about $1.54 billion through Medicare, Medicaid, and CHIP and about $148 million in federal grants and cooperative agreements. The coverage figure includes federal and state shares of Medicaid and CHIP payments and spans many health services. GAO reports no abortion-only amount in those totals. A claim would need to identify the service, the payer, and which funds were used to establish what paid for a particular abortion. GAO findings.

What the law controls

  • Whether care is legally available. In 2022, Dobbs overturned Roe and Casey and held that the U.S. Constitution itself confers no right to abortion. State statutes and constitutions now play a central role in determining access. Dobbs opinion.
  • Which services public money can cover. In 1980, Harris v. McRae upheld the Hyde Amendment and held that participating states were not required to fund abortions for which Congress withheld federal reimbursement. The majority accepted selective public funding in support of childbirth. Dissenters argued that withdrawing support placed a distinct burden on Medicaid patients who could not afford care themselves. Harris majority and dissents.
  • Which providers can receive program payments. Section 71113 of Public Law 119-21 barred federal Medicaid payments for services furnished by certain entities for one year beginning July 4, 2025. The definition included certain tax-exempt family-planning providers that offered abortions beyond the Hyde exceptions and whose relevant federal and state Medicaid payments exceeded $800,000 for fiscal year 2023 services. The restriction could also reach payments for contraception or screenings. In a separate 2025 case, Medina held that Medicaid’s free-choice-of-provider provision gives patients no private right enforceable through 42 U.S.C. § 1983. Public Law 119-21, § 71113, Medina opinion.

Public support and moral responsibility

One justification for limiting subsidy is that government has an obligation to protect prenatal life and can direct public money toward childbirth. A justification for coverage is that the ability to act on a legal choice should depend less on income. A provider-wide exclusion raises an additional question because it can interrupt care for people seeking other services. Each argument depends on a judgment about what the state owes to the pregnant person, the fetus, and the people paying for public programs. Harris majority and dissents, The case for bodily autonomy and the parental-duty reply.

Medical education raises the same question. Accredited OB/GYN residencies must provide clinical experience in abortion or access to it, including support for training in another state where local law prevents it. Residents may opt out. Before Dobbs, 140 of 286 programs (49%) offered routine abortion training, based on Ryan Program status or program acknowledgment. ACGME requirements, Vinekar and colleagues.

The other side of the ledger. Training in abortion builds skills that clinicians also use to manage miscarriage and obstetric emergencies. Taxpayers fund many things people object to on conscience, including war. This project reports funding by policy and state. ACGME requirements and FAQs.

Legal and accounting questions

U.S. federal law prohibits knowingly transferring human fetal tissue for valuable consideration when the transfer affects interstate commerce. Reasonable payments for specified activities, such as transportation, processing and storage, are excluded. The January 2026 NIH notice bars its funding for covered research using fetal tissue from elective abortions. The notice preserves funding eligibility for tissue from miscarriage and stillbirth under existing requirements. 42 U.S.C. § 289g-2 · NIH funding notice

To test whether a clinic made money from transferring tissue, match the contract and invoices to payments received and documented costs for the same work and period. An invoice shows what was billed. A payment record shows what changed hands. Aggregate revenue and list prices leave the question of profit open.

Public investigations and open questions

Public agencies publish different parts of a case. An FTC docket can contain a complaint, a court finding, and later orders. An FDA warning letter records the agency’s concerns at a given date. A recall and a public-health investigation can name a product and follow it across distributors and states.

Cases with enough records to follow

  • FTC and Georgia v. Stem Cell Institute of America. The FTC case file brings together the 2021 complaint, a 2024 summary judgment finding deceptive advertising, and court orders for a permanent marketing ban and monetary relief. The court credited $40,270 in earlier refunds and ordered $3,310,146 in restitution. The next evidence to look for is a disbursement or collection record showing how much of that order reached customers. Case docket and exhibits · Complaint · Summary judgment · Injunction · Monetary order · FTC refund index
  • Iowa v. Biologics Health and Summit Partners Group. A subpoena response identified at least 175 Iowa customers who paid at least $1,649,080.50. The April 2026 judgment ordered reimbursement of that amount for the identified consumers and created a process for additional claims. The records also document direct mail and sales seminars, giving an example of how the sellers reached consumers. The judgment leaves later collection and total sales outside Iowa open. Findings and judgment, pp. 30, 134, 148–149 · Attorney general’s account
  • Genetech and Liveyon, 2018 recall. FDA and CDC records identify Genetech as the processor of umbilical cord blood products sold through Liveyon. CDC’s first report counted 12 infections in three states. The follow-up investigation identified 20 infections in eight states, including the original cases. Laboratory results linked patient and product samples and pointed toward contamination during processing. This establishes a supplier and product in one outbreak. The follow-up gives no national complication rate across stem-cell treatments. FDA warning letter · Recall notice · Initial report · Follow-up investigation

What the Nygård records establish

Charge: The 2020 U.S. indictment charged Peter Nygård with racketeering, sex trafficking and related offenses. Court finding: In a separate Canadian case, an Ontario appellate court left four sexual assault convictions and an 11-year sentence in place in 2026. Allegation: In 2022 a Manitoba legislator alleged that a proposed company used cells from aborted fetuses in his treatments. The debate records the allegation. The U.S. indictment and Canadian decisions supply no evidence identifying a tissue source or tracing a transfer or payment to a clinic or broker. U.S. indictment · Canadian extradition docket · Ontario appeal decision · Manitoba debate

Party statement: Canadian Press reported that Nygård’s bail affidavit described stem-cell injections four times a year in China and Panama. That is his account of his treatment. It provides no independent evidence of what was administered or where it came from. What to look for in the next file

  • Who supplied the product? Look for invoices, product labels and a lot identifier that connects processor, distributor and treatment. A shared marketing term gives no verified link between suppliers.
  • Who sold, referred and got paid? Separate the clinic, seminar organizer, processor and distributor. Contracts and invoices can establish which entity received a fee. Gross receipts leave costs and profit open.
  • What happened after enforcement? Tracing a complaint through judgment, appeal, recall completion or refund disbursement shows where an action stopped. Track the case number and date so multiple updates do not become multiple cases.
  • How often did harm occur? A case series can show a real injury. To estimate its frequency, look for the total number of treated patients and comparable follow-up. Look for records of benefit using the same treatment and patient population.
  • What will public records miss? Agency actions reflect what was reported, investigated and made public. Look for earlier names and related entities where filings document the connection. Geography and the absence of a published case cannot establish market size or safety.

The economics of abortion pills and instruments

Companies that sell abortion medications and procedural instruments have a financial stake in how products are approved, priced and purchased. Patients and families face different stakes, including what they can afford and what support they would have if the pregnancy continued. The ethical question is whether law and funding protect the people who bear those consequences. Bodily autonomy and parental duty · Public funding.

Costs that show up elsewhere

Patients and households. A 2019 survey of 675 patients at four clinics found median anticipated out-of-pocket costs of $189 for in-state care and $712 for out-of-state care, including nonmedical costs. Those results describe the surveyed patients before Dobbs. The study of denial and household finances also found greater financial distress after denial near clinics’ gestational limits. Patient spending study, Table 2.

Follow-up care. In a study of 2011–2012 California fee-for-service Medicaid claims, 4% of medication abortions were followed by an abortion-related emergency department visit. The visits included assessment without treatment. Across all abortion methods in the study, the median total reimbursed cost for the abortion and related follow-up was $941 when follow-up took place at an emergency department and $536 when it took place at the original site. California Medicaid claims study.

Processing and disposal. A 2023 civil settlement required Danco to pay $765,000 to resolve federal allegations involving country-of-origin marking and customs duties on Mifeprex made with imported ingredients. Danco denied the allegations and the agreement recorded no admission of liability. In its original approval record, the FDA evaluated manufacturing emissions, pharmaceutical waste and excretion and found no significant environmental impact under the assumptions it reviewed. A current estimate would need current volumes, disposal data and measured releases. The pregnancy-remains records concern a separate disposal practice. Signed DOJ settlement · FDA environmental assessment and finding.

    Exploitation, markets and legal oversight

    Who can profit when someone else loses control over a pregnancy or what happens to its remains? The records show where law allows payments, where authorities acted against abuse, and where oversight has missed people. A payment can be legal and still raise a moral question about what should be bought or sold. An act can be illegal while institutions fail to recognize the people it harms.

    When traffickers control a pregnancy

    In a U.S. study published in 2014, researchers recruited 107 sex trafficking survivors through service providers and shelters. Of the 34 who answered the question about abortion coercion, 18 reported that at least one abortion had been at least partly forced. Some described direct pressure from traffickers. The figure counts respondents who reported coercion. The study’s recruitment method and the small number of answers limit what it can tell us about how widespread the abuse is. Lederer and Wetzel, pp. 66, 72–74.

    Federal law criminalizes sex trafficking involving force, fraud or coercion and also covers commercial sexual exploitation of minors without proof of those means. The Government Accountability Office reported that federal officials described some state and local systems penalizing trafficked children without identifying them as victims. CDC abortion surveillance does not request trafficking status or coercion, and federal trafficking collections cannot estimate U.S. prevalence. The number of abortions coerced by traffickers remains unknown nationally and by county. 18 U.S.C. § 1591, GAO child trafficking report, Bureau of Justice Statistics, CDC surveillance methods.

    Fetal tissue sales and reimbursement

    Federal law prohibits certain transfers of fetal tissue for payment while excluding reasonable payments for specified expenses from that prohibition. The 2017 California civil judgment above documents unlawful sales by two bioscience companies. It leaves open what clinics received and whether any reimbursement exceeded actual costs. Those questions require transaction and cost records. Financial and court records, 42 U.S.C. § 289g-2.

    Waste-to-energy disposal

    In 2014, the UK Department of Health told Parliament that a Channel 4 investigation had brought to its attention the use of waste-to-energy incinerators to dispose of fetal remains. The department called the practice unacceptable and NHS England wrote to trusts. The parliamentary record documents disposal and the government response. It provides no payment records establishing that remains were sold as fuel. UK Parliament, April 8, 2014, Channel 4 records deposited with Parliament.

    Australia’s national waste report describes incinerators that handle mostly medical waste without recovering energy. That distinction matters when reading historical disposal records. New South Wales record · Australian energy recovery report

    Vaccine manufacturing and school requirements

    FDA labels identify longstanding fetal-derived cell lines used in vaccine manufacturing. The rubella component of M-M-R II uses WI-38 lung fibroblasts, and the Varivax label discloses residual DNA and protein from MRC-5 cells. California school-entry rules generally require MMR and varicella vaccination, subject to exemptions. The rules specify the vaccinations, and the labels describe manufacturing. Neither record identifies payments to abortion providers. M-M-R II label, Varivax label, California school-entry regulations.

    What patients may have to pay

    Household finances after denial

    Miller, Wherry, and Foster linked Turnaway Study participants to credit records and compared women whose pregnancies were just below or just above a provider’s gestational limit when they sought care. Financial trajectories were similar before the encounter. On average, those who were denied care experienced more financial distress for several years afterward. The comparison concerns people near the limits in the study. It provides evidence about household costs while leaving the ethical questions about prenatal life and bodily autonomy open. Study and published version.

    What changes when abortion policy changes?

    The financial stakes extend beyond a sale or a clinic bill. A policy can change how many pregnancies continue, when care occurs, how far people travel, and who pays. Those effects matter to a policy that claims to protect prenatal life, bodily autonomy or both. The studies below examine different populations and outcomes.

    Public coverage changes who pays and can change how many abortions occur

    After Illinois began state-funded Medicaid abortion coverage in 2018, researchers comparing it with 29 other states estimated 2.43 more abortions (95% CI 1.31 to 3.55) and 1.66 fewer births (95% CI 0.15 to 3.18 fewer) per 1,000 females aged 15 to 44. The study used 2014–2021 data. A separate interrupted time-series study found lower patient payments at participating Illinois facilities. Medicaid paid for care that some patients would previously have paid for themselves. The effect on the state budget also depends on reimbursements and changes in care use. Illinois comparison study, Exhibit 1.

    Rules can shift where and when care happens

    Researchers estimated that Tennessee’s 2015 two-visit, 48-hour waiting requirement increased the share of abortions occurring in the second trimester by 53% to 69% relative to the estimated share without the rule. Their estimate of the effect on the overall abortion rate was less conclusive. After Dobbs, mandatory reporting from North Carolina showed an increase of about 38 abortions a day for out-of-state patients in the following year. That study counted medication and procedural abortions reported in North Carolina. Interstate care and clinician-provided telehealth can move care across jurisdictions, so a decline in abortions performed in a state cannot by itself measure how many abortions its residents obtained. Tennessee waiting-period study · North Carolina reporting study.

    More births, household finances and medical spillovers

    A 2025 analysis of 14 states adopting complete or six-week bans estimated 22,180 births above its modeled expectation through 2023. This is a short-run estimate for those states. At the household level, the Turnaway Study credit-record analysis estimated about $1,750 more debt at least 30 days overdue among people denied wanted abortions near clinic gestational limits. These results describe different populations. They cannot be multiplied together to estimate nationwide household debt. Births study · Credit-record study.

    In a 2026 qualitative study, 40 physicians across nine states with total bans described delays in miscarriage and other pregnancy-related care, as well as additional consultations and transfers. The interviews identify ways that clinical and administrative costs can rise. They do not establish how often those delays happened. A separate 2026 study estimated 5.5% higher neonatal mortality in 16 states after bans (95% credible interval 2.6% to 8.5%). Excluding Texas reduced the estimate to 2.2%, with an interval that included zero. The study’s overall estimate for deaths before age one was also inconclusive. Physician interviews · Neonatal and infant mortality study · Publisher’s summary.

    What an economic estimate includes

    A 2026 study modeled $339.6 million in societal costs in the first year after bans took effect in 14 states. It allocated $233.2 million to pregnancies carried to term, $104.7 million to travel for out-of-state in-clinic care, and $1.7 million to pregnancies ending in miscarriage or stillbirth. Those figures come from a model of how people responded to the bans and the costs of those outcomes. They depend on the study’s assumptions and measure costs in dollars. Platt and colleagues’ cost model.

    A dollar paid by an insurer is also a dollar received by a provider. For a public budget, count the change in program spending. For a household, count payments, travel, child care and time away from work. To estimate the cost to society, account for the resources used and avoid counting the same payment twice. The study estimates here answer narrower questions than the tracker’s ethical argument.

    Community, heritage and the economy

    Select a country with published estimates on the map to see its estimated births and abortions. A nation is a chain of people handing down language, skills, obligations and memory. Where fewer births mean fewer people in the next generation, the effects can reach workplaces, neighborhoods and families. Population aging can strain pension systems and shrink workforces, and migration can help fill some labor shortages. World Bank on pensions and aging, World Bank on migration.

    Families and communities can keep a culture alive and carry its language, skills and memory into exchanges with others.
