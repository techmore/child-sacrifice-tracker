A social and philosophical inquiry

Decimation killed one soldier in ten.
We end three pregnancies in ten.

Every age faces hard choices when urgent needs and obligations to vulnerable life collide. This project sets the modern abortion debate beside the historic record of child sacrifice and asks what a society owes its next generation.

Decimation, tripled

Roman decimation was a punishment imposed on a military unit. One soldier in ten was selected to die, sometimes at the hands of fellow soldiers. The word still carries the horror of a community made to pay for its failure of virtue. Taylor on Roman decimation.

Decimation took one life in ten within a punished unit. WHO estimates that 29 in 100 pregnancies worldwide ended in induced abortion in 2015 to 2019. The thesis this project explores is that a society can lose a generation at scale without ever feeling it as a loss, because the loss is private, dispersed and unnamed. WHO abortion estimates.

Where the comparison strains. Commanders imposed decimation on unwilling soldiers. Abortion is a personal decision shaped by particular circumstances. Whether an embryo or fetus has the moral standing of a soldier is the very question in dispute.

The record of sacrifice

Some accounts describe child sacrifice as an appeal for rain or divine favor. This project asks what that history can tell us about obligations to vulnerable life when present needs press.

  1. Carthage, first millennium BCE. The Carthage Tophet held urns containing cremated remains of infants and young children. Researchers disagree over how much of the evidence points to sacrifice and how much is consistent with natural deaths. Smith and colleagues, Schwartz and colleagues.
  2. Ancient Israel. Leviticus forbids offering children to Molech, and Jeremiah condemns child sacrifice at Tophet in the Valley of Hinnom. The texts leave open how often such offerings occurred. Leviticus 18, Jeremiah 7.
  3. Huanchaquito-Las Llamas, Peru, 15th century. Excavators found 137 children, three adults and more than 200 camelids. The research team suggested that the sacrifice may have been a response to severe rains and flooding. Prieto and colleagues.
  4. Tlatelolco, Aztec Mexico. A study published in 2008 reported that excavations at a temple dedicated to the Aztec god of wind and rain recovered the remains of 43 sacrificial victims, 37 of them children or adolescents. De La Cruz and colleagues.
Where the comparison strains. The children in these cases had already been born. Most abortions reported in U.S. surveillance occur early in pregnancy. The modern debate also asks whether the state may require someone to remain pregnant. CDC abortion surveillance.

The strongest case against this page

The comparison with historic child sacrifice puts the focus on the life that ends. Pregnancy also requires the sustained use of another person's body. Even if a fetus has the same right to life as a born person, the bodily autonomy argument asks whether that right entitles it to remain inside someone's body against their will.

Judith Jarvis Thomson asks you to imagine waking up connected, without your consent, to an unconscious violinist with a kidney ailment. Your kidneys will keep him alive while he recovers. If you disconnect before nine months, he will die. Thomson argues that you may disconnect even though the violinist has a right to life. The analogy is especially forceful when pregnancy results from rape. For consensual sex, Thomson imagines an airborne seed that can grow into a person taking root in your home despite a protective screen. Her point is that taking a foreseeable risk can leave your right to refuse bodily support intact. A Defense of Abortion.

In the United States, living organ donation generally requires consent, even when a life depends on it. In McFall v. Shimp, a Pennsylvania court refused to order a compatible relative to donate bone marrow. The autonomy argument calls for the same protection against compelled bodily support during pregnancy. Federal informed consent regulation, Court decision.

Pregnancy and childbirth can cause severe bleeding and lasting harm. When the membranes rupture very early in pregnancy, waiting can increase the risk of serious infection. How to manage the pregnancy depends on the medical circumstances and the patient's preferences. A fetal diagnosis can bring a prognosis of death before or soon after birth, sometimes with substantial uncertainty. The case for autonomy gives the person facing these risks and decisions the authority to decide what they can bear. Maternal health risks, Early rupture of membranes, Life-limiting fetal diagnoses.

The parental-duty reply holds that parents owe their children care beyond what strangers owe each other. If that duty begins before birth, gestation may count as the basic care a fetus needs at that stage. The responsibility reply makes a separate claim. When people voluntarily take a foreseeable risk and thereby bring a dependent life into existence, they can acquire duties even if they tried to prevent pregnancy. That argument relies on a voluntary act, so it falls away in cases of rape. Some pro-life thinkers still oppose abortion after rape because they see the fetus's claim to protection as independent of how conception occurred. In medical emergencies, some argue that treatment aimed at saving the pregnant person's life can be permissible even when fetal death is a foreseeable consequence. Parental obligation and responsibility, The Ethics of Abortion, The doctrine of double effect.

Don Marquis argues that a central wrong of killing is that it deprives someone of the relationships, experiences and projects their future would have held. If a fetus already has a future like ours, abortion deprives it of that future. For Marquis, this gives a strong moral reason against abortion without first having to establish fetal personhood. Why Abortion Is Immoral.

This project takes seriously the possibility that the parental relationship creates duties before birth. Gestation calls for a degree of bodily support that ordinary caregiving rarely does, and adoption can transfer care only after childbirth. Any case for compelling pregnancy in law must explain why that duty justifies the burdens it places on the person who is pregnant and how clinicians can exercise judgment when a pregnancy threatens health or life.

What remains unresolved. What moral claims does prenatal life have at different stages? Can a parental duty require gestation, and how should rape, a life-limiting fetal diagnosis or serious health risks affect that duty? Even where a moral duty exists, when may the state enforce it, and who should decide how much medical risk is too much?

Family, fragility and the thing we cannot measure

The strongest evidence here concerns connection. A meta-analysis of 148 studies and over 300,000 people found that those with stronger social relationships had 50% greater odds of survival. One British panel study found that married participants reported higher life satisfaction after accounting for their well-being before marriage. Holt-Lunstad and colleagues, Grover and Helliwell.

Supportive marriages and extended families can give people someone to share a shock with and someone willing to tell them the truth. Friendships, work and community commitments can also help people learn to bear hardship. The case this project makes is that supportive family ties can build resilience under stress.

What families and communities teach

Family life can teach people how to keep a promise when keeping it is inconvenient. In the Politics, Aristotle treats the household as an association that meets everyday needs. In the Nicomachean Ethics, he argues that moral virtue develops through practice. Taken together, these ideas suggest why the ordinary work of raising children, caring for elders, and making amends matters. Aristotle defended male authority and slavery within the household. His account of how habits form can also be put to work in households whose members have equal standing. 1, 2

The Confucian Analects treats care and respect for parents as part of moral cultivation. It also counsels people to raise objections gently when they disagree with their parents. In Reflections on the Revolution in France, Edmund Burke describes political society as a partnership that joins the living with their predecessors and with people yet to be born. That view asks us to consider what future generations will inherit alongside what people need today. 3, 4

In Suicide, Émile Durkheim argued that weaker integration into family, religious, and political groups helped explain differences in suicide rates across populations. Alexis de Tocqueville saw voluntary associations as a way for people in a democracy to learn to act together. In his 1995 essay Bowling Alone, Robert D. Putnam documented declines in many forms of American civic participation in the late twentieth century. A bowling league illustrates what is at stake. Meeting regularly gives people a chance to build trust and learn who will show up when needed. 5, 6, 7

These ties can help people absorb a shock. Nassim Nicholas Taleb calls a system antifragile when it gains from variation or stress. Take a household where an illness prompts relatives and neighbors to share school pickups. If the arrangement leaves the household with more people it can call on in the next crisis, it illustrates antifragility. If the same work repeatedly falls to one exhausted caregiver, the household may have coped while leaving that person more vulnerable. 8

Susan Moller Okin argues that assigning most unpaid care to women can curb their earning power and leave them dependent on a partner. John Stuart Mill's On Liberty warns that social pressure can restrict freedom and argues that preventing harm to others is what justifies coercion of adults. A defense of family life must reckon with who bears the costs of care and leave room for people to dissent from the roles others expect them to fill. 9, 10

These questions carry into the abortion debate. Many opponents of abortion see protecting prenatal life as a duty to a vulnerable human being. Many defenders of abortion access emphasize bodily autonomy and the burdens of being compelled to continue a pregnancy. An ethic of care across generations leaves open how to weigh those claims in law. It gives people on both sides a reason to ask who will share the work of caring for children and what makes that care possible.

Public money and public conscience

Should people be taxed to pay for something they believe is the taking of a life? Since fiscal year 1977 the Hyde Amendment has largely barred federal Medicaid funds from paying for abortion. Its exceptions cover rape, incest and danger to the pregnant patient's life. Congress restored the rape and incest exceptions in 1993. States may fund broader coverage with their own revenue. Congressional Research Service, KFF state policy analysis.

Medical education raises the same question. Accredited OB/GYN residencies must provide clinical experience in abortion or access to it, including support for training in another state where local law prevents it. Residents may opt out. Before Dobbs, 140 of 286 programs (49%) offered routine abortion training, based on Ryan Program status or program acknowledgment. ACGME requirements, Vinekar and colleagues.

The other side of the ledger. Training in abortion builds skills that clinicians also use to manage miscarriage and obstetric emergencies. Taxpayers fund many things people object to on conscience, including war. This project reports funding by policy and state. ACGME requirements and FAQs.

Why people say they choose it

Any serious account of virtue has to meet the person in the waiting room. In a study published in 2013 of 954 women seeking abortion in the United States, most gave more than one reason. Biggs and colleagues.

Community, heritage and the economy

Select a country with published estimates on the map to see its births, abortions and a simple economic thought experiment. A nation is a chain of people handing down language, skills, obligations and memory. Where fewer births mean fewer people in the next generation, the effects can reach workplaces, neighborhoods and families. Population aging can strain pension systems and shrink workforces, and migration can help fill some labor shortages. World Bank on pensions and aging, World Bank on migration.

Families and communities can keep a culture alive and carry its language, skills and memory into exchanges with others.
