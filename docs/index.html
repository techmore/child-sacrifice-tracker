A social and philosophical inquiry

Decimation killed one soldier in ten.
We end three pregnancies in ten.

Every age decides what it will give up for the life it wants. This project sets the modern abortion debate beside the historic record of child sacrifice, and asks what a society owes its next generation.

Decimation, tripled

Decimation was the Roman army's punishment for a unit that broke faith: one man in ten was killed, often by his own comrades. The word still carries the horror of a community made to pay for its failure of virtue.

The modern figure is nearly three times that proportion. The thesis this project explores is that a society can lose a generation at scale without ever feeling it as a loss, because the loss is private, dispersed and unnamed.

Where the comparison strains. Decimation was imposed by commanders on unwilling men, and historians consider it rare. Abortion is usually a personal decision made under particular circumstances. Whether an embryo or fetus has the moral standing of a soldier is the very question in dispute.

The record of sacrifice

Ancient peoples gave up children to secure rain, harvest, victory or favor. The parallel worth examining is the logic: a present good purchased with a future life.

  1. Carthage, first millennium BCE. Urns of cremated infants fill the sanctuaries called tophets. A 2014 scholarly team called the evidence for sacrifice "overwhelming." Others argue many of the infants died naturally.
  2. Ancient Israel. Leviticus forbids offering children to Molech, and Tophet, in the Valley of Hinnom, became a byword for that horror. The prohibition itself shows the practice was a live temptation.
  3. Huanchaquito-Las Llamas, Peru, 15th century. Excavators found 137 children and 3 adults with 200 camelids, possibly offered during catastrophic rains.
  4. Tlatelolco, Aztec Mexico. Excavations at Temple R, reported in a 2008 study, recovered 43 sacrificial victims, 37 of them children, at a temple tied to the rain and wind gods.
Where the comparison strains. Sacrificed children were born, or were killed as a public religious rite. Modern abortion has no deity or ritual, and most occur early in pregnancy. Many readers will find the analogy clarifying, and many will find it deeply wrong. Both reactions are part of the inquiry.

America across fifty years

Line shows Guttmacher national estimates, 1973 to 2020. The #WeCount project separately estimated 1,126,760 abortions in the formal US health system in 2025, with 22% of that total imputed. Its method differs, so it is shown as a separate point.

The number of US abortions peaked around 1990 (the rate peaked around 1980), fell for almost three decades, rose after 2017, and held roughly steady from 2024 to 2025. Worldwide, the rate dipped slightly into the early 2000s and has since returned to its early-1990s level, while the share of unintended pregnancies ending in abortion rose from 51% to 61%.

A finding that cuts across both camps. Abortion rates are similar where it is broadly legal (40 per 1,000 women) and where it is restricted (36 per 1,000), though excluding China and India the rate is higher where it is restricted. These comparisons suggest that legal status alone does not determine the number. Unintended pregnancy is the stronger driver.

Family, fragility and the thing we cannot measure

The strongest evidence here concerns connection. A meta-analysis of 148 studies and over 300,000 people found that those with stronger social relationships had 50% greater odds of survival, an association the authors called comparable with quitting smoking. Married people also report higher life satisfaction, and one major British panel study found that this advantage persists after accounting for how happy people were before marriage.

This is the case for antifragility. A person embedded in marriage, children, parents and cousins has people who will absorb a shock with them, and people who will tell them the truth. A person who curates their whole environment meets little resistance, and so never builds the capacity to bear it.

What the evidence does not show. The US suicide rate rose from 2000 to 2018, dipped in 2019 and 2020, and returned to about 14 per 100,000, but the global rate fell by about a third while abortion stayed common. No data here shows that abortion causes suicide or unhappiness. Researchers also still debate how much of marriage's benefit comes from happier people being likelier to marry. The link proposed here is a hypothesis about social structure, offered for argument.

Public money and public conscience

Should people be taxed to pay for something they believe is the taking of a life? Since 1977 the Hyde Amendment has largely barred federal Medicaid money from paying for abortion. Its current exceptions cover rape, incest and danger to the woman's life. The rape and incest exceptions were dropped in 1981 and restored in 1993. States may go further with their own revenue.

13states ban abortion

Medical education raises the same question. Accredited OB/GYN residencies must provide clinical experience in abortion or access to it, including in another state where it is unlawful locally, and residents with religious or moral objections may opt out. Before Dobbs, an estimated 140 of 286 programs (49%) offered routine abortion training, based on Ryan Program status or program acknowledgment.

The other side of the ledger. The same training teaches management of miscarriage and obstetric emergencies, so removing it has clinical costs. Taxpayers also fund many things others object to on conscience, including war. This project reports funding by policy and state. It does not trace money or training to named hospitals, universities or doctors.

Why people say they choose it

Any serious account of virtue has to meet the person in the waiting room. In a study of 954 women seeking abortion, most gave more than one reason.

40%financial reasons
36%timing
31%partner-related reasons
29%need to focus on other children

Read through this project's thesis, those answers describe missing structure: too little money, an absent partner, no family nearby to share the load. People on every side of the debate could work on that together.

Community, heritage and the economy

Select any country on the map to see its births, abortions and a simple economic thought experiment. A nation is a chain of people handing down language, skills, obligations and memory. Each missing link is also a missing worker, neighbor, parent and caregiver. Aging countries feel this first, in strained pensions and in reliance on migration to fill the gap.

This gives an unusual argument for both heritage and global cooperation. Every culture's survival depends on its own families, and a world of secure, continuing peoples has more to exchange.

Why the GDP figure is only a thought experiment. It multiplies abortions by output per person. Many women who have an abortion go on to have children later, so not every abortion means one fewer lifetime birth. Childbearing also affects women's education and earnings, which this simple figure leaves out.

Pregnant and deciding

  • Abortion Finder lists verified US providers and current state law.

Continuing a pregnancy

  • WIC provides food and health support for mothers and children.
  • 211 connects you to local housing, food and childcare help.
  • Maternal Mental Health Hotline: call or text 1-833-852-6262, 24 hours a day.

In crisis

  • Call or text 988 for the Suicide & Crisis Lifeline.
  • Call 911 if you or someone else is in immediate danger.

Make your voice heard

  • Find your elected officials.
  • Find your state legislators and follow their bills.
  • Testify at public hearings, write to local papers, vote, and support families directly.
Why this site has no list of clinics or staff. Abortion providers have faced murder, arson and threats. In Planned Parenthood v. American Coalition of Life Activists, a divided federal appeals court upheld a finding that "GUILTY" posters naming abortion providers, together with listing those plaintiffs on a website, were true threats. Disagreement belongs with lawmakers and in public argument. Never approach, harass or threaten patients or staff.
